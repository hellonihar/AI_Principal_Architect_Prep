# 9. Your interview-ready closing statement

> Part of Expectation 4: Provide architectural leadership for capabilities, agentic workflows, AI orchestration, evaluations, AI observability, and AI governance

My approach to AI architecture leadership is to build an enterprise AI ecosystem that is reusable, scalable, observable, and governed by design.

I would establish common AI capabilities such as model access, retrieval, prompt management, document intelligence, and agent tooling. I would define standard patterns for agentic workflows and orchestration, ensuring that AI reasoning is combined with deterministic business processes, enterprise APIs, and human approval wherever necessary.

I would also make evaluation and observability first-class architectural capabilities. Every AI solution should have measurable quality criteria, traceable execution, operational monitoring, cost visibility, and continuous improvement mechanisms.

Finally, I would embed AI governance across the lifecycle, covering data protection, model risk, agent permissions, responsible AI, auditability, and human accountability.

The objective is not simply to introduce more AI solutions. It is to establish an enterprise architecture that enables business units to adopt AI faster while maintaining consistency, operational reliability, security, and measurable business value.

## 10. How to position your existing architecture experience

Your application architecture and Appian/BPM background gives you a useful way to explain this responsibility.

The strongest connection is combining intelligent decision-making with controlled enterprise process execution.

You can explain the relationship this way:

- Your application architecture experience is relevant to defining application boundaries, integration contracts, reusable services, and non-functional requirements.
- Your BPM experience is relevant to deterministic orchestration, process state, human approvals, exception handling, and audit trails.
- Your enterprise architecture experience is relevant to reference architectures, governance, reusable patterns, and cross-team adoption.
- Your AI architecture development should demonstrate how you extend these foundations with model integration, RAG, agent orchestration, AI evaluation, and observability.

The distinction to emphasise is that agentic AI does not replace enterprise architecture or BPM. It introduces an intelligent reasoning capability into an enterprise architecture that still needs to preserve process control, security, accountability, and operational discipline.

My recommendation: Be particularly strong on the distinction between an AI agent, an orchestration framework, and a BPM/workflow engine. It is one of the most important architectural discussions behind this job expectation.
